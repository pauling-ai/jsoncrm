JSONCRM vs HubSpot

The short version: HubSpot is a complete revenue platform — CRM, sequences, forms, marketing automation, reporting — with a genuinely useful free tier and a cost curve that bends upward the moment you grow or want AI. JSONCRM is none of that: it's a pipeline of structured records in your git repo with agents as first-class users and a flat price of zero. If you need the machine, HubSpot earns its money. If you mainly need a pipeline you and your agents operate, paying seats to track your own leads reads strange fast.

HubSpot is the incumbent most teams try first, and often the last one they leave — the free CRM is real software, not a demo. The honest framing isn't "HubSpot bad"; it's that HubSpot's economics are built for a marketing organization, and its data model is built for HubSpot. Both matter at the edges.

The core difference: platform gravity vs file portability

Everything in HubSpot lives in HubSpot: contacts, deals, timelines, email logs. Export exists, but the living data — workflows, sequences, history — doesn't travel. The product is excellent at being the center of gravity; that's by design. JSONCRM inverts it: the center of gravity is a directory of JSON files. Any tool can read them, any agent can write them (through enforced commands), any git host backs them up. You can leave JSONCRM in one git clone because there's nothing to leave — the data was always yours, in the open.

The AI angle sharpens the difference. HubSpot's AI (Breeze and friends) is metered in credits on paid tiers — you rent their intelligence against your data. JSONCRM's agent story is bring-your-own: Claude, Cursor, Codex, anything speaking MCP, unlimited calls, every write previewable with dry-run. When agents got cheap in 2026, "seats + credits for AI on your own data" became a hard sell.

🧭 Choose HubSpot if…

  • You want the whole machine: sequences, forms, chat, marketing automation out of the box
  • Non-technical sales teammates need polish and mobile apps
  • You value vendor-integrated reporting and attribution over raw data ownership
  • Your budget fits seats-and-credits economics comfortably

⚡ Choose JSONCRM if…

  • Your pipeline is structured records agents should operate, with dry-run previews
  • Zero marginal cost as the pipeline grows matters more than built-in marketing
  • You want git diffs, revert, and PR review as the audit trail
  • Data ownership and portability are requirements, not preferences
  • Your "reporting" is honestly five numbers — jsoncrm stats covers it

A note on fairness

Feature-for-feature, HubSpot wins on width — that's what 15 years and billions in R&D buy, and pretending otherwise would be marketing. JSONCRM wins on a different axis on purpose: ownership, cost-at-scale, and being natively operable by the AI agents you already pay for. Plenty of healthy companies run both: HubSpot for the marketing org, a git-native CRM for the founder-led pipeline that feeds it.

HubSpot alternatives — common questions

Is HubSpot's free CRM really free?

The core CRM — contacts, deals, tasks — is free and good. The economics kick in at the edges: sequences, automation, custom reporting, and meaningful AI usage live on paid tiers, priced per seat (plus credits for AI), and the free tier's contact notifications exist to upsell. It's a fair deal for many teams; just price the version you'll actually run, not the signup page.

Can AI agents work directly in HubSpot?

Via HubSpot's API with tier-based rate limits, and via Breeze, HubSpot's own AI, metered in credits. JSONCRM gives agents a first-party MCP server — 21 CLI-parity tools, unlimited calls, dry-run previews on every write — because the pipeline is local files, not a hosted product.

How do I migrate a pipeline out of HubSpot?

Export contacts and deals as CSV, map lifecycle stage to your pipeline stages (leads/prospects/customers or your own), map fields to flat record properties, and drop the arrays into your stage files. Run jsoncrm deduplicate on the result, then validate, then commit — from that point the audit log is git.
