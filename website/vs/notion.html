JSONCRM vs Notion as a CRM

The short version: Notion is an excellent workspace with a database bolted in — and thousands of people run a perfectly fine lightweight CRM on a Notion template. JSONCRM is a purpose-built pipeline with enforced stages, an audit log, and agents as first-class users. If your CRM is a board next to your docs, stay in Notion. If your CRM is a structured dataset that software and agents operate on, the template starts fighting you.

This comparison is a little unfair to both products, because they barely overlap. Notion answers "where do my notes, docs, and wiki live?" — and its databases are flexible enough to hold contacts. JSONCRM answers "where does my pipeline state live?" — and deliberately does nothing else. The interesting question is which one your "CRM" actually is.

The core difference: schema by convention vs schema by enforcement

A Notion CRM is a database you configured: properties named Stage, Score, Last contact — and a convention that everyone (and every agent) respects them. Nothing enforces the convention: a stage can be renamed, a select option deleted, a row left half-filled. JSONCRM's .crm.json defines stages, transitions, identity, and valid scores — promote physically cannot move a lead to customer, and an invalid score is rejected at write time. Convention reads nicer; enforcement scales.

The same split shows up in history. Notion gives you page history (on paid plans). Git gives you git log -p on every record, forever, plus branches and PRs for review — the machinery software teams already trust for exactly this problem.

🧭 Choose Notion if…

  • Your CRM is lightweight contact tracking living beside real docs and wikis
  • Non-technical teammates need real-time editing and familiar views
  • You want rich pages per account — meeting notes, specs, links — not structured fields
  • You already pay for Notion and the team lives there

⚡ Choose JSONCRM if…

  • The pipeline is structured records agents should compute over — scores, stages, bulk updates
  • You want enforced transitions and validation instead of convention
  • Git as the audit log (diffs, revert, PR review) fits your team
  • You want unlimited, unmetered agent access without API rate limits
  • Your data should live in your repo, not a vendor's cloud

A note on fairness

Notion does things JSONCRM doesn't attempt and never will: docs, wikis, real-time collaboration, a gallery view worth screenshotting. Plenty of two-person startups run healthy pipelines on a Notion board and are right to. The failure mode we're addressing is specific: teams whose Notion CRM grew into a dataset that humans re-type, agents can't reliably touch, and nobody can export cleanly. If that's you, welcome.

Notion CRM — common questions

Can AI agents use Notion as a CRM?

Partially. Notion exposes an API and MCP integrations, but the data model is pages and blocks, not pipeline records — agents end up parsing layout instead of reading state, and the API is rate-limited. JSONCRM gives agents structured records and 21 purpose-built MCP tools with dry-run support, unmetered, because the files are local.

Is Notion a good CRM for small teams?

For lightweight pipelines — contacts, a status board, notes — yes; it's flexible and your team probably already knows it. It stops fitting when you need enforced stage transitions, field validation, an audit trail, or programmatic/agent access, which is exactly the gap a structured, git-native CRM fills.

How do I move a CRM out of Notion?

Export the database as CSV, map columns to JSONCRM fields (name, company, email, score, stage), and drop the arrays into leads.json / prospects.json / customers.json. Because JSONCRM records are flat objects with arbitrary fields, any column can come along. Then validate with jsoncrm validate and commit — your history starts on day one.
